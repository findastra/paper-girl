for every screen" (platform-platypus#14).

## The idea (Astra)

Draw an article, get a cool summary on a one-of-a-kind collectible card with its own drawing. People keep their cards.
Every card also goes into Astra's library on her home server, so the next person who asks for that subject and level
gets a card that already exists instead of paying to make it again. Works for every level, from curious to PhDs having
their morning coffee.

## How Claude decided the open details

**Fresh card or library card.** When someone draws, Patty first looks in the library for a card of that subject and
level that this person has not seen.
- Levels 1-2 (common): always a library card when one exists; a new one only when none is left.
- Level 3: a library card about 7 times in 10.
- Levels 4-5 (rare): a new paper more often, which is what makes them rare.
- A daily budget of new cards (set by Astra, from the API costs). When it runs out, everyone gets library cards until
  midnight. Nobody ever waits on a broken card: on any failure they get a library card.

**Authors who want their card changed.** An "I wrote this" button on every card. The author proves it one of two ways:
1. Sign in with **ORCID** (the free ID most researchers have; papers list their authors' ORCID iDs). If the iD is on
   the paper, they are verified at once.
2. Or a code sent to the **corresponding author's email printed on the paper** (never an address they type in).
Then they suggest changes; Patty rewrites the card and Astra (or Patty, by a rule Astra sets) approves. The card then
shows "Checked by an author ✓". Degrees and schools are not checked: being on the paper is what matters.

**Texts.** Sending texts from a server needs a texting service (Twilio or similar), a registered US sender (carrier
approval takes days to weeks, with fees), people's written opt-in, and STOP handling. Picture texts cost a couple of
cents each. Astra makes that account herself; Patty sends from the home server so Astra sees every card sent.
"Send to a friend" needs none of this: it uses the visitor's own phone.

**Cloudflare, in plain words.** Cloudflare is a company that sits between websites and the internet for security and
speed, and it also rents small servers. We only need its free **Tunnel**: a program on the home server that dials out
to Cloudflare, so the public website can reach Patty's server without opening any door in Astra's router. Astra makes
the free account herself. (Tailscale Funnel does the same job, also free.)
