# Paper Girl card plan

Astra's answers and the decisions she left to Claude, 2026-11-13. Deck: "Paper Girl: the card plan" (private
artifact). Drafts: [card-drafts-20261113.html](card-drafts-20261113.html) (front and back, three rarities) and
[card-designs-20261113.html](card-designs-20261113.html) (four looks). Layout follows Mirror Pool's "build a website
for every screen" (platform-platypus#14).

## The idea (Astra)

Draw an article, get a cool summary on a one-of-a-kind collectible card with its own drawing. People keep their cards.
Every card also goes into Astra's library on her home server, so the next person who asks for that subject and level
gets a card that already exists instead of paying to make it again. Works for every level, from curious to PhDs having
their morning coffee.

## Card rules, 2026-11-14 (Astra)

- **Front:** subject icon, name, LV; the picture (a perfect square); a stats row of real facts only (subject, read
  time, where it was published); a band with the rarity word and difficulty stars. No symbol before the rarity word,
  no headline, no links, no made-up moves or stats.
- **Back:** the three sections, "Read the paper", the card number. "✍ Approved by its author" appears only when it is
  true; nothing is said when the authors have not seen it.
- **Rarity:** levels 1-4 = Common, Uncommon, Rare, Holo rare, from how hard the paper is. **Legendary (gold) = a card
  its author approved or revised.** The stars still show how hard the paper is.
- **Look:** cute but not girly; simple; no stars, sparkles or filler.

## Card rules, later on 2026-11-14 (Astra)

- **Names:** about three words, two adjectives and a noun, in real science words that recall what the paper did,
  not childish ones (the card is cute, the words are not). The first five: Discerning Solitary Salticid, Babbling
  Sac-winged Pup, Facilitative Weed-clipping Pika, Synesthetic Pupillary Seven, Statistical Neonatal Listener.
  The name is centered at the top.
- **Stats:** Type (a colored circle with the subject's symbol, always the same per subject, like a trading card's type:
  Psychology = pink brain, Biology = green leaf, Chemistry = amber flask, Earth science = blue globe, Physics = coral
  atom, Math = teal sigma), Paper (Open ↗), and Read (a tick box the reader checks, kept in their browser). No read
  time and no journal on the front.
- **Level and difficulty:** the level number sits inside a star in the top corner (the rarity color; gold on gold cards); the band at the bottom says the rarity and "Difficulty" with its stars. The tick box is labeled "Read paper" (honor system, no effect on rarity, never flips the card).
- **Gold card:** Legendary or verified by its author = the whole card is gold whatever its subject, with sparkles over
  the whole card; the Type circle still shows the subject.
- **Sparkles:** the four-point twinkle from Astra's portfolio portrait (findastra.github.io): rainbow on Holo rare,
  gold on gold cards. A sheen sweeps across a card while it flips.

## The page, 2026-11-14 (Astra, deck mode)

- **Not girly any more.** The page is mostly gray; gold stays the brand accent (logo, "My deck", "Card of the day").
- **Card of the day** is the big box at the top: the paper's full write-up on the left (everything the card leaves
  out: the three sections, small print, source details, the original paper), the card on the right, and "Your next
  rabbit hole" inside the same box. For now it rotates through papers that already have cards.
- **Type:** Times New Roman for every word on the page, cards included, like a white paper (Astra, 2026-11-14; the
  typed-paper box was tried and dropped: the box is gray like the page). No sparkle by the logo or the motto; the motto
  is centered in the space under the card.
- **Footer:** Astra's unified footer, copied from Spoonfed (By Astra, sparkle, star, sparkle, Find Out More, GitHub).
- **Accents follow the card's subject color** (a biology card makes the box's line, section titles and buttons green).
- **Motto** under the card, the site's little logo line: "Every card, one idea wiser." (Implies: the more cards you
  collect, the smarter you get.)
- **All cards are shiny:** a soft light that only brightens, never changes colors; it follows the mouse like a lamp on
  a real card and otherwise drifts slowly at a different pace on each card. Rainbow and gold cards add sparkles.
- **Card number at the top of the front**, under the name, big enough to read; the back has no link.
- **Back footer:** the day the card was made (`made`). On an author-approved card, the author's own signature or
  their lab's logo as a round stamp, which they send with their answer (placeholder stamp for now).
- **No "Card of the day" date row and no "Today's paper" link** above the box; the box is a shade lighter than the page.
- The name is not decided ("White Paper Cards" and others under discussion).

## Drawing rules for Paper Patty's pictures (OpenAI)

One picture per paper. Pick **one specific, real thing the paper is about** (a mitochondrion, an amoeba, a jumping
spider, a newborn in an EEG cap), preferably one the library has not drawn yet, and draw only that, cute: simple
shapes, flat colors, one dark outline, a small friendly face where it fits, a plain soft background. No stars, sparkles,
planets, plants or other filler unless the paper is about them. Square picture. Never text in the picture except a
word the paper is about (like the bat's "ba!").

**Style (Astra, 2026-11-14): E, retro Saturday-morning cartoon** with a hand-painted background showing where the subject really lives in its paper (`drawing.scene`). Picked from 25 OpenAI drafts in five styles (`docs/art-styles-20261114.html`). This replaces the plain-background rule above: cartoonish, a real setting, still no stars, sparkles or text.

The first five cards (`lib/data/sample-deck-20261114.json`) use real eLife papers, one per rarity: Spider Memory
(Common), Babbling Bat (Uncommon), Tiny Gardener (Rare), Color Number (Holo rare), Pattern Listener (Legendary, marked
author-approved as a demo; no author has been asked). Their pictures were drawn by OpenAI (gpt-image-2) on 2026-11-14 with
`scripts/draw-card-art-20261114.mjs` and Paper Patty's key `PAPERGIRL_PATTY_OPENAI_API_KEY`: 720 px WebP in
`public/art/cards/`, 1024 px originals in `art-originals/cards/`.

6. **Author gift:** an author who approves or fixes their card gets (a) a printed gold Author's Edition stamped
   "Author's copy", mailed to the address they reply with, plus its digital twin, and (b) 10 digital cards, two of each
   level, two of them other authors' Author's Editions. A fix gets the same gift as "Looks good".
7. **Wave-1 email** (no server yet; authors answer by replying). Subject: "Your paper is now a Paper Girl card. Did we
   get it right?" It says up front that Paper Patty is an AI card writer, shows the card back, and offers "Looks good",
   their fix (one round) or "Take it down"; then the gift, the PO box, and "Reply stop and we will never email you again."

**Still open there** (recommended in brackets): Q7 send 100 at once or 20 a day [20 a day: the 20-email hand test,

## Drawing guide: every hero is a living thing with eyes (decided 2026-11-14)

Astra (drawing Q1, in the Card Strategy session): "The item always needs to be a living thing with eyes. So even
abstract stuff, just make shit up." Every card's one hero is a creature with eyes, in every subject, and Paper Patty
may invent one. The STYLE line stays style E. To write a card's `drawing.subject`, ask in order and stop at the first yes:
1. Is something alive in the paper? Draw that exact organism, cell or plant, with cartoon eyes if it has none.
2. Can you see or hold the thing the paper is about, at any size? Turn that real thing into a creature with eyes.
3. Is the idea a shape? Draw a creature whose body is that shape.
4. Otherwise invent a creature that lives in the everyday scene the card's hook uses.

Never draw real people or the authors, equations or words, filler planets, stock beakers, atoms or brains, logos, or
anything scary. Check the library first: no two cards share a hero. Write one sentence: the creature, its true look,
what it is doing, and the paper's real setting around it. Viruses and parasites get the friendly style too.

Examples: knot theory → a rope snake with big eyes tied in a trefoil knot. Superconductors → a frosty cube creature
with big eyes and tiny feet floating above a magnet in a chilly lab. Water splitting → a crystal creature with tiny arms
pulling a water molecule apart. Drawing Q2 (may the author's one fix include one redraw) is still open.

Sources: only CC BY papers qualify (many arXiv papers are not CC BY); Q9 on adding sources is still open.

## How Claude decided the open details

**Fresh card or library card.** When someone draws, Patty first looks in the library for a card of that subject and
level that this person has not seen.
- Levels 1-2 (common): always a library card when one exists; a new one only when none is left.
- Level 3: a library card about 7 times in 10.
- Levels 4-5 (rare): a new paper more often, which is what makes them rare.
- A daily budget of new cards (set by Astra, from the API costs). When it runs out, everyone gets library cards until
  midnight. Nobody ever waits on a broken card: on any failure they get a library card.

**Every paper goes to its authors (Astra, 2026-11-13).** Every paper that gets cards is sent to its authors, who get
**one shot**: approve it, revise it once, or take it down permanently.
- **Who:** the corresponding author's email printed on the paper (Europe PMC lists it for most open papers). Never an
  address someone types in. The email itself proves who they are: its private link works only from that inbox.
- **When:** once per paper, when its first card is made, not once per card or level. One email shows all the paper's
  cards (every level) and covers them all.
- **Cards don't wait.** A new card is live at once, marked "Not yet seen by the authors". Authors are slow or never
  answer, and waiting would make drawing no fun.
- **The emails wait in a queue** (Astra, 2026-11-13); nothing about drawing or keeping cards waits for them.
- **Their answer, through the link:** *Looks good* or *Revise once* (they edit the text; Paper Patty rewrites every
  level from their edit; the paper is then locked). **Either way every card of that paper becomes an Author's
  Edition** (Astra, 2026-11-13: "the golden one for if the actual author reviewed it and approved it is amazing"): the
  rarest kind, gold background with drifting gold sparkles and an "Author's Edition" seal, whatever its level.
  **Everyone who already owns one gets it in their collection, with a notice: "✨ Your card was approved by its
  author" or "✨ Your card was updated by its author."** *Take it down* → the card leaves the library, owners keep a
  "retired by its author" placeholder (no text, no picture), and Patty never draws that paper again. No answer in
  30 days → the card just stays "not yet seen".
- **Courtesy, not permission:** the papers are CC BY, which already allows this; asking is how we keep authors happy.
- **Also needed:** an email-sending service and a domain of Astra's own to send from (github.io can't send email),
  plus a "never email me again" link in every message. Volume is one email per paper, so it stays small at first.
- An "I wrote this" button stays on every card for authors who find their card before the email arrives; it sends the
  same link to the paper's own email address. (ORCID sign-in is no longer needed.)

**Texts.** Sending texts from a server needs a texting service (Twilio or similar), a registered US sender (carrier
approval takes days to weeks, with fees), people's written opt-in, and STOP handling. Picture texts cost a couple of
cents each. Astra makes that account herself; Patty sends from the home server so Astra sees every card sent.
"Send to a friend" needs none of this: it uses the visitor's own phone.

**Cloudflare, in plain words.** Cloudflare is a company that sits between websites and the internet for security and
speed, and it also rents small servers. We only need its free **Tunnel**: a program on the home server that dials out
to Cloudflare, so the public website can reach Patty's server without opening any door in Astra's router. Astra makes
the free account herself. (Tailscale Funnel does the same job, also free.)

## Papermon, night of 2026-11-14 (Astra)

- **Name:** the game is **Papermon** (capital P), replacing Paper Girl everywhere. Logo and tagline still to choose.
- **Three reading levels:** the card is the kid-simple version; the paper beside it is a medium-level write-up of about
  two minutes (three sections of a few paragraphs each, `paper` in the card data, written from the publisher's own
  digest); the original article is the full science.
- **The paper:** 01 Strange hook, 02 What they found, 03 Why it matters (title lined up with 01 and 02, the side line
  only beside its body), then the study's small print, then **04 Bibliography**: the article cited in MLA 9th edition
  (EasyBib's default), with a hanging indent and the full article's read time under it. Starter cards carry an exact
  entry from Crossref (`cite`); other papers get one built from Europe PMC's data.
- **Nothing on the gray box but the paper and the card.** Every link is a button under the draw controls: Draw a card
  (shiny gold, full width), then Order my deck, How to play Papermon, Where the science comes from, More information.
  The box has the same 28 px gap all round and between the paper and the card; the card and buttons stay in view
  while the long paper scrolls.
- **Draw controls:** subject circles, reading-difficulty stars, and published-year bands (This year, 1-5, 5-10, 10-15,
  15-20, 20+ years).
- **The card front:** name at the top (no number there); stats Subject, Article (the journal's name, linked) and
  Completion (Read / Unread, a shiny gold circle), a second row Published (year) and Minted (year), then the card
  number lower down. Common and Uncommon written in black; Rare (silver), Holo rare (rainbow) and Legendary (gold) in
  shiny colored text. The stars are **Reading difficulty**. Sparkles turn half as fast as before.
- **Card numbers** go up by one in the order cards are minted: the five starter cards are No. 000,001 to 000,005.
- **The card back** has "Read the write-up" (opens it on the paper up top) and "Original article" buttons. Clicking a
  card anywhere flips it.
- **Your deck** opens with a tally: cards per subject (the symbol only; hover for the name), cards read, and the
  minutes of reading those read articles add up to.
- **How to play Papermon** pop-up: the goal is to read as many papers as you can; how to draw; three ways to read;
  rarity; subjects; your deck.
- **Logo and motto:** "Papermon" and the motto much bigger, shiny gold with a black outline.
- **Rarity score (decided and built, 2026-11-14):** Astra asked for "our own type of rank for rarity ... a number
  score ... all the stuff added up, weighted", with a chart. Points: 20 per reading-difficulty star (10 at first; see Polish below), 1 per year since
  the paper came out (no limit), 50 when the paper's author has checked the card. Score 0–29 Common, 30–49 Uncommon,
  50–69 Rare, 70–89 Holo rare, 90+ Legendary (the whole card gold). The score shows on each card's band; How to play
  has the chart (points, a colored scale, the score table, examples). The numbers live in one place (`SCORE`,
  `TIERS` in components/my-deck-20261114.tsx). This settles "author-approved = Legendary vs gold finish": the author's
  check adds 50 points. LV and the stars stay the reading difficulty.
- **Logo (decided):** the card fan, all three cards gold, no mark on them, black outline
  (`public/papermon-logo-20261114.svg`), before "Papermon" in the header and as the browser-tab icon.
- **Year choice and draw odds (replaces the year bands the same night):** the picker offers only **This year** and
  **Past years** (tap again for any year). Newer papers come up more often: left open, a drawn card's paper is from
  this year 30%, last year 20%, 2–5 years ago 20%, 6–10 years ago 15%, 11–20 years ago 10%, more than 20 years ago 5%
  (no limit on age). Past years uses the same odds without this year. The draw picks an age group by these odds among
  the groups that have a matching card, then a card in it; with no matching card it searches for a real paper from an
  age group picked the same way. How to play shows the odds. Numbers: `AGE_ODDS` in components/draw-bar-20261114.tsx.
  The journals searched today start in 2003 (open question Q9 on more sources).
- **Art:** the jumping spider (Common) needs more distinguishing detail; the others are good.
